The Extemporaneous Dispensatory

Hieronymus David Gaubius·London, 1741·English

Imprint
London: printed for W. Innys and R. Manby, 1741
Format
8vo · xii, 214 pp.
Language
English, translated from the Latin of the Leiden Libellus (1739)
Subjects
prescription-writing · compound medicines · dosage

A working manual of prescription-writing in the Boerhaavian manner: general rules for composing a formula, then the internal and external forms — boluses, draughts, electuaries, juleps, fomentations — each with worked examples and doses. It fixes mid-century compounding practice at the moment chemical preparations were crowding the older Galenicals.

Gaubius, Boerhaave’s successor in chemistry at Leiden, wrote for students who could recite the materia medica but not turn it into a defensible prescription. The examples run to familiar simples — myrrh in the stomachic electuaries, betony and rosemary among the cephalics — and the translator keeps the Latin formulas beside their English doses.

Design note — why tiers

Every work gets an overview URL, but not every work earns a page of prose. An importance assessment stored in the catalog record (tier) drives treatment richness. The template degrades by dropping whole sections, never by shrinking them — so a minimal entry reads as complete, not broken. Promotion is a data edit, not a redesign.
